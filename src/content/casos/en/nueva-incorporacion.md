## The business challenge

In a direct sales model, growth doesn't come from selling more: it comes from **more people joining and staying**. Incorporación — the process by which a Director brings a new Consultant into her personal group — is that front door. It's the moment a business is born inside Yanbal.

The problem: the process had been designed around the company's operational logic (requirements, credit validation, documentation) rather than around the experience of the two people who actually live it — the Director who invites and the Consultant who decides to join.

> **The starting question:** Why does a Director with every motivation to grow end up enrolling far fewer people than she could, and why does a genuinely interested candidate drop off halfway through?

## My role

I led the research and the design of the new experience as Product Designer, connecting the findings with corporate teams, country stakeholders, and the development team currently building it.

## The process

I designed the research in two layers, to avoid settling for either the official version of the process or isolated anecdotes:

**1. Desk research and internal interviews.** Mapping the process as the company believes it works, with corporate stakeholders and country leads. This is where the gaps between the documented process and each market's real process surfaced.

**2. Field research in Colombia and Peru.** Two markets with different maturity and behavior, chosen to separate the structural from the local:
- **20+ Directors** across all career levels.
- **30 Consultants**, deliberately split between **enrollers and non-enrollers**.

That last decision was the most important one in the study: interviewing only those who enroll tells you how they do it, but never why the others don't. The contrast between the two groups was where the real signal appeared.

## From inherited assumptions to new paradigms

As a market leader, Yanbal held deeply rooted beliefs about why someone decides to join. Part of the work was **putting those beliefs to the test instead of assuming them**: some held up, others collapsed, and from that came the new paradigms the redesign was built on.

## Results

- New enrollment paradigms validated in the field and adopted as the foundation of the redesign.
- Product redesigned end to end based on evidence rather than intuition.
- **Development underway**: the new experience launches in Bolivia as the first market, with projected rollout across the region.

## What I learned

Designing a process that spans two people at once — the one who invites and the one who joins — forces you to stop thinking in screens and start thinking about the whole service. The biggest takeaway was methodological: **the sample you choose determines what you're able to discover**. Deliberately including the Consultants who *don't* enroll completely changed the diagnosis, and with it, the product.
